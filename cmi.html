01

Project overview

Connecting Mothers Initiative is a nonprofit that helps military mothers and caregivers find stability through every move. One in three military families relocates each year, and every move means rebuilding a support system from scratch: new doctors, childcare, benefits, community.

I lead UX and product design for the Resource Hub inside CMI The Circle, the organization’s app: a vetted directory of services, articles and community, built so a mother new to a base can find what she needs and trust it. I work with our founder, Director of UX and Product Manager, and mentor a UX intern.

  • 2.6M+Military family members
  • 1 in 3Families relocate every year
  • 59%Of military families report loneliness

Source: Connecting Mothers Initiative

02

Problem & goal

Problem

Military mothers and caregivers struggle to find trusted, relevant parenting resources. Information is scattered, overwhelming and rarely verified, which leaves them stressed and slow to decide, often mid-move.

Goal

Design one trusted place where they can find verified information, nearby services and community, filtered to their needs and clear about what to do next.

Ten supporting problems and ten user goals came down to four themes. Each one names what’s broken today and what the hub had to do about it.

  • Trust

    BrokenCan’t tell which sources are reliable; advice conflicts.

    Designed forVerified, organized content; reviews to compare options.

  • Scattered

    BrokenNo central place; information and services live apart.

    Designed forOne hub connecting information, services and events.

  • Overload & time

    BrokenToo many options, little time, unclear next steps.

    Designed forFilters, clear guides, save for later.

  • Isolated

    BrokenNot tailored to life stage; far from family.

    Designed forPersonal results, mothers’ stories, local events.

03

Research

Interviews

Five semi-structured interviews, one hour each on Zoom, with mothers aged 24–47 raising one to four children. The research ran before CMI narrowed its focus to military families, so participants were mothers and caregivers in general.

We coded every transcript in ATLAS.tiQualitative data analysis software, used to code all five interviewsatlasti.com ↗ with a shared codebook: 14 codes, each with a definition and “when to use / when not to use” rules, applied to 329 quotations. Trust came up far more than anything else.

  • Finding 01

    Trust comes from lived experience

    Parents trust other parents first, then check with doctors or research.

    “I would take the advice more from someone who I have known has gone through it.”P2

  • Finding 02

    The village is friends when family is far

    Without family nearby, friends become the support system.

    “Our village is … everyone close to us, especially within proximity.”P2

  • Finding 03

    Clear structure signals credibility

    Content sorted by topic and child’s stage feels trustworthy; clutter doesn’t.

    “It should be not very overwhelming … with a well-structured menu.”P4

Competitive analysis

We compared four organizations serving families. Each one owns a single need from the interviews, and none of them connects the four. That gap is where the Resource Hub sits.

  • FindhelpServices directory, but clinical, with no stories or community.
  • ZocdocFast and convenient, but healthcare booking only.
  • March of DimesTrusted expert content, but education rather than local services.
  • The Motherhood ProjectCommunity events, with little information or services.

Flows & structure

Every flow hinged on the same two questions, and our own walkthroughs showed users slowing down at the first one. So the design answers both on the results page, before Lisa has to open anything.

06

High-fidelity design: following Lisa

Lisa is new to San Diego and running the household largely on her own. She does everything from her phone, in a place she doesn’t know yet, so what matters most is trust. She can’t waste time, and she needs to know what she finds is reliable. We follow her through three flows.

Flow 1

Find a trusted service

  1. 01

    Local from the first tap

    Search is pre-set to Lisa’s ZIP code, with bookmarks and nearby events right on the home screen.

  2. 02

    Judge relevance without opening anything

    Every result shows category, CMI score, cost, distance, rating and a verified check.

  3. 03

    Trust, explained

    The CMI Certified badge and CMI Meter each open a short note on what was verified and when.

  4. 04

    One tap to act

    Call, Email, Website and Directions sit above the fold, and reviews come from other military families.

Flow 2

Find a trusted article

  1. 01

    Articles beside services

    Tabs for Services, Articles and Guides keep information and services in one search.

  2. 02

    Key takeaways first

    Each article opens with a short summary, so Lisa gets the answer in a five-minute break.

  3. 03

    Save for later

    Bookmark from the article and it waits on the home screen when the kids are asleep.

Flow 3

Leave a review

  1. 01

    A nudge at the right time

    After Lisa’s visit, a notification invites her back to share how it went.

  2. 02

    A light review sheet

    Stars, a title and a few lines, written for the next military family.

  3. 03

    Closing the loop

    Today’s user becomes the next family’s trusted source, the lived experience participants asked for.

Learnings

  • AI as a design multiplier

    Working with Claude Code through the Figma MCP sped up the hi-fi enough to prototype three full flows instead of one, and those flows drew the strongest feedback.

  • Research that survives a pivot

    Trust, overload and a village far from family held for military families. They just hit harder when you move every two to three years.
